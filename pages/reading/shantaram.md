Didn't realize that it was so long until I was a couple chapters in. But you warm up to the eccentric characters by the end of the first quarter, and then it's the story and the pacing that capture and keep you hooked for much of the rest of the book. The middle feels like filler. Then the pace picks up fast near the end, though the end itself feels abrupt.

The raw and vivid descriptions of the streets of [Bombay](/photos/mumbai-india-2026), paired with the intricacies of the Indian culture across the entire social-status spectrum were particularly interesting. The book is a mixed bag of genres as it progresses; there's history, humour, (often misplaced imo) philosophy, romance and a bit of politics with a drizzle of [geopolitics](/reading/prisoners-of-geography). The overall theme is that of the protagonist trying to flee his tragic past and in so doing, forging a new identity for himself in India by falling in love, living in the slums, partaking in the Bombay crime scene, fighting a war, and even hugging a bear :) Most of the characters in the book are well-written, Abdullah, Kadhar Khan, Karla, Johnny, Vikram, Modena, etc. Understandably there's bound to be multiple arcs in a book this long but unfortunately there were a few that fell flat and felt out of place. For instance, the bear hugging and the whole deal with the war in Afghanistan. But then again, if it's really based on a true story, it makes it that much more intriguing. Though the claim of this being a semi-autobiographical account is not strong enough.

I listened to most of this book while running which goes on to show how easy to follow this book is. Sure, there were times when I had a hard time recalling a character by name but it wasn't too bad. If it weren't so long with a lot of what I felt like was filler, it would've been much more memorable and enjoyable. All in all, a long and decent read.
